Both views tell the same story: in every European country, the gap between the best and worst women's team is far larger than in the men's league, which already points to talent in women's football being concentrated in a few clubs rather than spread widely.

Cutting each men's league to the size of its women's league makes the disparity even clearer. A full men's league has six to eight extra teams at the bottom, and the teams above them pile up goal difference in those extra games against easier opponents, which stretches the men's gap and makes it look closer to the women's than a like-for-like comparison would. Once the men's leagues are cut to the same size, with goal difference counted only in games among those top teams, the European men's gap falls from about 2.8 to about 1.9 goals per game, while the women's gap stays at about 5.3. The women's gap goes from roughly 1.9 times the men's to about 2.7 times.

Note: MLS match results aren't in the dataset, so the cut MLS figure uses the top teams' full-season goal difference rather than games among themselves.
